## Clinical/semantic spot review

Automated provenance labels are not reliable enough to stand in for semantic review. The following production outputs are release-blocking examples:

1. **Wrong certified approach:** response 939, `Anterior total hip arthroplasty`, resolved to `tha_posterior` and returned five posterior-approach questions. Because the packet is certified and scored highly, the current quality gate does not catch this dangerous identity mismatch.
2. **Prompt intent ignored:** response 902, `cement spacer for infected TKA`, returned the same 15-question primary-TKA packet as routine `TKA`. It contains no infection/spacer-specific preparation.
3. **Direct user question ignored:** response 911 asks how proximal tibial and distal femoral cuts affect gaps. The packet contains the tibial answer, but returns 15 generic TKA questions instead of leading with or directly answering the requested concept.
4. **Compound-case contamination:** response 923, `Total hip and total knee arthroplasty`, resolved to `tka` but its sole pimp question concerns pudendal neuropraxia during hip arthroscopy. The item is nevertheless labeled `rag`, `direct`, and directly supported.
5. **Incorrect subtype mapping:** response 943, `Subacromial decompression arthroscopic with open biceps tenodesis`, resolved to `distal_biceps_tenodesis_proximal`. Its questions are actually about the proximal long-head biceps, exposing a canonical-slug naming/mapping mismatch.
6. **Over-aggressive clarification:** ordinary high-value inputs such as `Distal radius fracture`, `Tibial plateau fracture`, and `Supracondylar humerus fracture` are deliberately withheld pending operative/nonoperative clarification. That behavior is defensible for safety, but it creates a zero-value first response for common CasePrep usage and accounts for a substantial part of the 36 zero-question packets.
7. **Resolver coverage gaps:** clearly operative prompts including `Triceps tendon repair`, `Subtrochanteric fracture IM NAIL`, `Posterior fusion for scoliosis`, `Menisectomy`, and `volar ganglion cyst removal` remained unresolved and were withheld.
8. **Known procedure intermittently unavailable:** three identical `Carpal tunnel release` rows ended as unavailable after approach clarification, while `Endoscopic carpal tunnel release` succeeded. The generic prompt should reliably offer usable approach choices without being counted as a broken packet.
9. **Unsupported generation remains visible:** six packets were `generated_fallback`; examples include MPFL reconstruction, tibial IM nailing, posterior cervical fusion, and olecranon ORIF. These questions have zero citations and are explicitly marked `unsupported`, but still reach the user.

## Recommended improvement order

1. Add a hard compatibility check between approach/modifier terms and the selected canonical packet before certified content can pass (anterior/posterior, primary/revision/infection, anatomic site, and procedure subtype).
2. Resolve or explicitly disambiguate compound prompts before retrieval; never let one procedure's retrieval item pass as `direct` for another.
3. Expand aliases for the real unresolved operative prompts in this cohort, starting with subtrochanteric IM nail, triceps/patellar tendon repair, meniscectomy, posterior scoliosis fusion, ganglion excision, and glenoid/proximal-humerus fixation.
4. Replace the terminal fracture-management clarification with a useful two-track response or a one-click default to operative preparation when CasePrep context and ORIF-adjacent language support it.
5. Make prompt-specific concepts influence ranking, not only canonical procedure identity. Infection/spacer, approach, hardware, fracture pattern, and explicit questions should rerank or filter the retrieved bank.
6. Do not display unsupported generated fallback as normal content. Either add sources, visibly separate it, or withhold it until it passes the same grounding bar.
7. Add these exact failures as regression tests and rerun this 100-case audit after each resolver/retriever change.

## Interpretation boundary

This automated audit measures resolution, output completeness, provenance metadata, citations, and operational reliability. It does not prove factual clinical correctness. The JSON artifact preserves the generated pimp questions and answers for targeted expert review, without user identifiers.
